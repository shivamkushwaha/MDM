Decision Theory Lab

Edit the payoff matrix, change the decision alternatives or states of nature, and compare decision rules under uncertainty and risk. All calculations update automatically.

01 · Problem definition

Decision context and assumptions

Set the objective and the unit used to report payoffs or costs.
For a payoff or profit problem, higher values are preferred. The criteria and regret calculations are oriented accordingly.
02 · Editable input

Payoff matrix

Rows represent decision alternatives; columns represent possible states of nature. Enter positive or negative values.
Probabilities are used for EMV and perfect-information calculations. Criterion weights are separate inputs used only by the weighted-average rule. Both sets of weights are normalized automatically when their totals are non-zero, so they do not have to add to exactly 100%.
03 · Decision criteria

Decision-rule settings

Adjust the Hurwicz optimism coefficient. The other rules use the current matrix and the inputs above.
0 = pessimistic · 1 = optimistic
Hurwicz: for maximization, α × best payoff + (1 − α) × worst payoff. For cost minimization, the same expression is applied to the best (lowest) and worst (highest) costs, and the lowest Hurwicz value is selected.
04 · Results

Decision analysis summary

The preferred alternative under each rule is highlighted. Ties are retained rather than arbitrarily broken.

Recommended alternative by criterion

Alternative-wise criterion scores

Maximax / Maximin use the best / worst outcome of each alternative. Minimax regret minimizes the largest opportunity loss. Laplace gives equal weight to all states. Weighted uses the editable criterion weights. EMV uses the probability inputs.
05 · Opportunity loss

Regret (opportunity-loss) matrix

Regret is the gap between the best outcome available in a state and the outcome from the selected alternative.
For maximization: regret = best payoff in that state − alternative payoff. For cost minimization: regret = alternative cost − lowest cost in that state. The minimax-regret rule chooses the smallest row maximum.
06 · Perfect information

Expected value of perfect information (EVPI)

Assumes the decision-maker learns the state of nature before choosing an alternative.
For each state, perfect information selects the best alternative for that state. The probability-weighted payoff (or cost) is then calculated across states.
For a maximization problem: EVPI = expected value with perfect information − best EMV without perfect information. For a cost-minimization problem: EVPI = best expected cost without information − expected cost with perfect information. EVPI is the maximum amount worth paying for perfect information.
07 · Formula guide

How to interpret the criteria

Decision under uncertainty

Maximax: optimistic rule; choose the alternative with the best possible payoff.
Maximin: conservative rule; choose the alternative with the best worst-case payoff.
Minimax regret: choose the alternative whose maximum regret is smallest.
Laplace: assume all states are equally likely and compare average outcomes.
Hurwicz: combine best and worst outcomes using α.

Decision under risk

Weighted criterion: use the user-entered state weights to calculate a weighted average.
EMV: use the state probabilities to calculate expected payoff or expected cost.
Expected value with perfect information: choose the best alternative separately in each state, then take the probability-weighted value.
EVPI: the expected improvement from having perfect information before deciding.
