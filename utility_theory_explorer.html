Utility Theory Explorer

Expected monetary value treats every rupee or dollar alike. A decision maker who cannot afford a large loss does not. This page shows how to measure a decision maker's attitude to risk with a lottery, turn payoffs into utilities, and choose by expected utility.

2. Measuring Utility with a Lottery

Risk premium = expected value of the lottery at the indifference point minus the sure payoff. A positive premium marks a risk avoider, a negative premium a risk taker, and zero a risk neutral decision maker.

3. The Utility Curve

The coloured curve joins the assessed utilities. The dashed straight line is the risk neutral benchmark. A curve that bends above the line (concave) belongs to a risk avoider; one that sags below it (convex) belongs to a risk taker.

5. Exponential Utility and Risk Tolerance

When assessing every payoff by lottery is impractical, a common shortcut is the exponential utility function U(x) = 1 − e−x/R, where R is the risk tolerance. A small R means strong risk aversion; a very large R approaches risk neutrality. The certainty equivalent (CE) is the sure amount worth the same as the risky alternative.

Certainty equivalent of each alternative as R grows. The preferred alternative is the highest line at the chosen R; crossing points are where the decision switches.
